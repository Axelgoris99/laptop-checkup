Dead pixel test

Full-screen colors, one after another, to spot dead pixels, stuck pixels, burn-in and blotches. Works on any laptop, monitor, TV or phone.

Buying a used laptop? The screen is one of 17 checks in the free Laptop Checkup: keyboard, battery wear, ports, company locks and more, with a report you can copy at the end.

How to use it

  1. Wipe the screen: dust looks a lot like a dead pixel.
  2. Turn brightness to the maximum.
  3. Press Start screen test. Click or press → for the next color, ← to go back, Esc to stop.
  4. Look at each color from where you'd normally sit, then up close, edge to edge.

What each color shows

  • White: dead pixels (black dots) and a yellow or blue tint.
  • Black: stuck pixels that stay lit, and light leaking from the edges (backlight bleed). Best in a dark room.
  • Grey 50%: burn-in. On OLED screens, look for a faint ghost of the taskbar, icons or windows.
  • Grey 25%: blotches and uneven, cloudy areas.
  • Red, green, blue: pixels showing the wrong color.
  • Gradient: visible steps instead of a smooth fade (banding).

Questions

What's the difference between a dead and a stuck pixel?

A dead pixel never lights up, so it shows as a black dot on white. A stuck pixel is stuck on one color, so it shows on black and on the other colors. Stuck pixels sometimes come back after a while; dead pixels don't.

How many dead pixels are acceptable on a used laptop?

One near the edge is a reason to ask for a lower price. Several, a cluster, a line, or anything in the middle of the screen is a reason to walk away: replacing a laptop screen often costs a large part of a used laptop's price.

Is burn-in fixable?

No. OLED burn-in is permanent wear. If you can see the taskbar ghost on the grey screen, it will only get worse.
